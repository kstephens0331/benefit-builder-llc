## How Section 125 lowers payroll tax in Illinois

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, which lowers the wage base used to calculate several taxes at once. In Illinois this matters more than in many states because Illinois levies a flat 4.95% state income tax with no standard deduction, so that tax applies from the first dollar of wages. When an employee makes a qualified pre-tax election, the elected amount is removed from the wage base before federal income tax, FICA, and the 4.95% Illinois tax are calculated.

Each of those taxes stacks. Federal income tax is graduated, with 2026 single brackets of 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37% applied above a standard deduction of $16,100. FICA is a combined 7.65%: Social Security at 6.2% up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus Medicare at 1.45% with no wage cap. Employers and employees each pay the full 7.65% FICA share. Because a qualified Section 125 election leaves the wage base for FICA, federal income tax, and the Illinois 4.95% tax, the employee keeps more take-home pay and the employer reduces its matching 7.65% FICA liability on the elected amount.

The employer saving is straightforward to estimate. Suppose an employee elects $300 a month, or $3,600 a year, into a qualified pre-tax benefit. The employer FICA saving is the election multiplied by 7.65%, which is $3,600 times 0.0765, or $275.40 a year for that one employee. Multiply that across a roster and the payroll-tax reduction compounds. The employee, meanwhile, avoids FICA, federal income tax, and the flat 4.95% Illinois income tax on the same $3,600, so the value to both sides comes from the same single election. These figures are illustrative; actual results depend on each employee's wages, filing status, and elections. For a deeper walkthrough of the employer side, see [how FICA savings work for employers](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

For Illinois employers, the absence of a state standard deduction means the 4.95% applies broadly, so the pre-tax shelter a cafeteria plan provides reaches more of each paycheck than it would in a state with a large deduction or graduated low brackets.

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A Section 125 plan can shelter a defined set of benefits. Common qualified items include premiums for employer-sponsored health, dental, and vision coverage; health flexible spending accounts (FSAs) and dependent care FSAs; contributions to a health savings account (HSA) when paired with a qualifying high-deductible plan; and dependent care assistance. Group-term life within statutory limits may also qualify. Long-term care insurance generally does not qualify to run through a cafeteria plan. The list of eligible benefits is set by federal rules, not by the employer, so plan design has to follow them. For specifics on what qualifies, see [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).
