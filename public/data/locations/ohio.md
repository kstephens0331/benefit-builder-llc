## How Section 125 lowers payroll tax in Ohio

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets Ohio employers convert selected employee-paid benefits into pre-tax payroll deductions. When an employee makes a qualified election, that money comes out of pay before federal income tax, FICA, and Ohio state income tax are calculated. The wage figure that those taxes apply to shrinks, and both the employee and the employer pay less.

Ohio is a meaningful case because of how its state tax is built. Ohio applies a flat 3.5% state income tax with no standard deduction, so the state reaches wages from the first dollar earned. There is no exempt threshold to clear before the 3.5% kicks in, which means every dollar moved into a pre-tax election removes 3.5% of state exposure in addition to the federal savings. Federal income tax, by contrast, is graduated. For 2026 the single brackets run 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%, applied above a standard deduction of $16,100. FICA is a flat 7.65% total, made up of Social Security at 6.2% up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500 plus Medicare at 1.45% on all wages. The employer and the employee each pay that 7.65% separately.

A qualified pre-tax election lowers the wage base for FICA, for federal income tax, and for the 3.5% Ohio tax at the same time. The employer keeps its matching 7.65% FICA share on every dollar that runs through the plan, which is where the employer-side payroll tax savings come from.

Here is one clearly hypothetical example. Suppose an Ohio employee elects $300 a month, which is $3,600 a year, into a qualified pre-tax benefit. The employer's FICA savings on that election is $3,600 multiplied by 7.65%, or about $275.40 for the year on that single employee. The employee separately avoids their own 7.65% FICA, plus federal income tax at their marginal bracket, plus the 3.5% Ohio tax on the same $3,600. Multiply the employer figure across a full roster of participating employees and the recovered payroll tax becomes a real line item rather than a rounding error. The exact federal amount depends on each employee's bracket, but the employer FICA portion is predictable because the 7.65% rate is fixed below the Social Security wage base. For a fuller walkthrough of the employer mechanics, see [how FICA savings work for employers](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A range of common benefits qualify for pre-tax treatment under a Section 125 plan. Insurance premiums for health, dental, and vision coverage can run pre-tax. Health care flexible spending accounts (FSAs) and dependent care FSAs qualify, as do dependent care assistance arrangements. Health savings account (HSA) contributions can also be made through the cafeteria plan. Long-term care insurance generally does not qualify for Section 125 treatment and is usually handled outside the plan. Getting the categories right matters, because an improperly classified benefit can undo the tax advantage. See [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125) for the detail.
