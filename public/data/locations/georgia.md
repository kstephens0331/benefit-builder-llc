## How Section 125 lowers payroll tax in Georgia

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets Georgia employers move qualified benefit elections out of taxable wages before payroll taxes are calculated. When an employee makes a pre-tax election, that money is deducted from gross pay and is never counted as taxable income, which lowers the wage base used for FICA, federal income tax, and Georgia state income tax all at once.

Georgia matters here because it imposes a flat 5.39 percent state income tax, the highest rate among the states Benefit Builder serves, applied after a standard deduction of $16,100. Federal income tax is graduated, with 2026 single brackets of 10, 12, 22, 24, 32, 35, and 37 percent and a federal standard deduction of $16,100. On top of income tax, FICA totals 7.65 percent: Social Security at 6.2 percent up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus Medicare at 1.45 percent with no wage cap. Both the employer and the employee each pay that 7.65 percent.

Because a qualified pre-tax election leaves the wage base for FICA, federal income tax, and the 5.39 percent Georgia tax, the employee keeps more take-home pay and the employer saves its full 7.65 percent FICA match on every dollar elected. Where Georgia stands apart is the comparatively high state rate: the same election shields income from a 5.39 percent state tax that lower-rate or no-tax states cannot match, so the employee take-home gain in Georgia is especially strong.

Here is a clearly hypothetical illustration of the employer side. Suppose an employee elects $300 a month, or $3,600 a year, into qualified pre-tax benefits. The employer FICA saving is the election multiplied by 7.65 percent, which works out to $275.40 a year for that one employee ($3,600 times 0.0765). Multiply that across a workforce and the recurring employer payroll tax savings add up quickly, while the employee separately benefits from lower FICA, lower federal income tax, and lower Georgia state income tax on the same elected amount. Actual figures depend on each employee's wages, filing status, and elections; this single number is only an example of how the math works, not a fixed monthly benefit or a promised result. For a deeper walk-through of the employer mechanics, see [how FICA savings work for employers](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

The takeaway for Georgia employers is that a properly administered cafeteria plan reduces payroll tax for the business and increases net pay for staff without changing the underlying benefit being purchased. The employer FICA savings recur every pay period for as long as the election stays in place, and they scale directly with participation, so the more employees who enroll in pre-tax benefits, the larger the cumulative payroll tax reduction for the business. ## What benefits can run pre-tax

A Section 125 plan can run a defined set of qualified benefits through pre-tax payroll. The common ones are insurance premiums (medical, dental, vision, and similar group coverage), health flexible spending accounts (FSAs), dependent care FSAs, health savings account (HSA) contributions made through payroll, and dependent care assistance. Each reduces taxable wages for the elected amount. Long-term care insurance generally does not qualify for pre-tax treatment inside a cafeteria plan and is typically handled separately. For a fuller breakdown of qualifying and non-qualifying benefits, see [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).
