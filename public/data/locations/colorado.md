## How Section 125 lowers payroll tax in Colorado

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, which shrinks the wages that payroll tax is calculated on. For Colorado employers, that matters on three fronts at once. Colorado levies a flat 4.4% state income tax with no standard deduction, so every taxable dollar is taxed the same way from the first dollar earned. Federal income tax is graduated, with 2026 single brackets of 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37% applied above a $16,100 standard deduction. On top of both sits FICA, which totals 7.65%: Social Security at 6.2% up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus Medicare at 1.45% with no wage ceiling. The employer and the employee each pay the full 7.65%.

When an employee makes a qualified pre-tax election through a cafeteria plan, that amount leaves the wage base used to compute FICA, federal income tax, and the 4.4% Colorado income tax. The employee keeps more take-home pay because fewer dollars are exposed to those taxes. The employer benefits directly too: because the election reduces taxable wages, the employer no longer owes its matching 7.65% FICA on that money. This is real money returned to the business on every payroll run, and it scales with participation.

Suppose an employee elects $300 a month, which is $3,600 a year, into qualified pre-tax benefits. The employer saves its share of FICA on that election: $3,600 multiplied by 7.65% equals $275.40 per year in reduced employer payroll tax for that one participant. Multiply that across a roster of participating employees and the savings compound quickly. The figures above are illustrative and depend on each employee's wages, filing status, and elections; they are not a guarantee, and they describe how the math works rather than a fixed benefit amount. ## What benefits can run pre-tax

A cafeteria plan can shelter a defined set of benefits. The most common are employer-sponsored insurance premiums, including medical, dental, and vision coverage. Health flexible spending accounts (FSAs) and dependent care FSAs both run pre-tax, as do contributions to a health savings account (HSA) when paired with a qualifying high-deductible plan. Dependent care assistance is also eligible under Section 125. Note that long-term care insurance generally does not qualify for pre-tax treatment inside a cafeteria plan. For a complete breakdown of eligible and ineligible items, review [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).
