## Why Laredo employers use Section 125

Laredo is the seat of Webb County, sitting on the Rio Grande at the United States and Mexico border. As one of the busiest inland ports in the country, Laredo runs an economy built on international trade, logistics and warehousing, healthcare, retail, and education. Goods crossing the border move through Laredo's customs brokers, freight carriers, and distribution centers, and that volume keeps a deep base of local employers staffing large payrolls year round.

When an employer carries a large local payroll, a Section 125 cafeteria plan becomes one of the most reliable ways to lower cost without cutting pay. A cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, which shrinks the wage base that both the worker and the employer are taxed on. Because Texas has no state income tax, Laredo employers do not lose any savings to a state withholding line. The savings come from two federal sources: federal income tax for the employee and the 7.65 percent FICA tax that both the employee and the employer pay. The employer share of that FICA reduction belongs to the business and recurs every pay period. Employers in nearby Rio Bravo and El Cenizo see the same structure, since the rules follow federal tax law rather than any local rate.

## Section 125 for Laredo's trade and logistics employers

Many Laredo employers run sizable hourly workforces in trade, logistics, and warehousing. These are exactly the workforces where pre-tax benefits make the clearest difference. Hourly employees feel every dollar of take-home pay, and a cafeteria plan raises that net pay by lowering the taxes withheld on each check, without the employer having to fund a raise.

High headcount is what turns this from a small benefit into a meaningful line on the books. The employer FICA savings apply per participating employee, so a Laredo distribution center or carrier with hundreds of workers sees those savings scale across the whole roster. The figure that drives the result is participation, and that is where administration matters. Benefit Builder runs the employee education that keeps participation strong, explaining the plan in plain terms so workers understand what they are electing and why their paycheck changes. Strong enrollment is what makes the FICA math work for a large employer rather than a handful of sign-ups.
