## How Section 125 saves Texas employers money

Texas has no state income tax. That single fact shapes how a Section 125 cafeteria plan works here. When an employee makes a pre-tax election under a cafeteria plan, the dollars come out of pay before two taxes are calculated: federal income tax and FICA. There is no third state layer to add, so in Texas the savings ride entirely on the federal and payroll tax pieces. Those pieces are substantial.

Federal income tax for 2026 single filers runs on graduated brackets of 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%, applied after a standard deduction of $16,100. FICA is a flat 7.65% total: Social Security at 6.2% on wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus Medicare at 1.45% on all wages. Both the employer and the employee pay 7.65%. So every dollar an employee moves into a pre-tax benefit is a dollar that is not taxed for FICA on either side, and a dollar that does not count toward the employee's federal taxable income.

The employer side of FICA is where Texas employers see a direct, predictable return. Because the employer pays 7.65% on every payroll dollar, lowering the FICA-taxable wage base lowers the employer's payroll tax bill automatically, with no change to the employee's take-home strategy beyond the election itself.

Here is a clearly hypothetical illustration. Suppose an employee elects $300 a month, which is $3,600 a year, into a qualifying pre-tax benefit. That $3,600 is no longer subject to FICA. The employer's FICA saving on that election is $3,600 times 7.65%, which equals $275.40 a year. Multiply that across a workforce and the numbers grow quickly. The employee also keeps more of each paycheck, because the same $3,600 is shielded from federal income tax at the employee's marginal bracket and from the employee's own 7.65% FICA share. None of this depends on a fixed monthly benefit amount; the savings scale with whatever the employee actually elects.

For a deeper walkthrough of the employer side of the math, see [how FICA savings work for employers](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A cafeteria plan lets employees pay for a defined set of benefits with pre-tax dollars. The common qualifying items are health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts and dependent care flexible spending accounts; health savings account contributions; and dependent care assistance. Long-term care insurance generally does not qualify for pre-tax treatment inside a Section 125 plan, which is a frequent point of confusion. The exact menu an employer offers is set in the plan document. For the full picture of what is eligible and what is not, see [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).
