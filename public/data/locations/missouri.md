## How Section 125 lowers payroll tax in Missouri

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees pay for certain benefits with pre-tax dollars. When an employee makes a qualified election, that money is removed from the wages used to calculate three separate taxes: FICA, federal income tax, and Missouri state income tax. The election happens before any of those taxes are figured, so the taxable wage base shrinks for both the worker and the company.

The Missouri math is straightforward because the state uses a flat rate. Missouri applies a 4.7% state income tax with a standard deduction of $16,100, so every pre-tax dollar an employee shifts into the plan is a dollar that the 4.7% never touches. Federal income tax is graduated, with 2026 single-filer brackets of 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37% and a standard deduction of $16,100, so the federal benefit depends on the employee's marginal bracket. FICA is a flat 7.65% total: Social Security at 6.2% up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus Medicare at 1.45% with no cap. Both the employer and the employee each pay that 7.65%.

This is where Missouri employers see a direct, repeatable saving. Because the qualified election is removed from FICA wages, the employer no longer owes its 7.65% on those dollars. The reduction is automatic and recurs every pay period the election stays in place.

Here is one clearly hypothetical example. Suppose an employee elects $300 a month, or $3,600 a year, into a qualified pre-tax benefit. The employer's FICA saving is 7.65% of $3,600, which is $275.40 for the year on that single employee. The employee also benefits on the same $3,600: those dollars avoid the employee share of FICA, avoid federal income tax at the employee's marginal bracket, and avoid the Missouri 4.7% state income tax. Multiply the employer side across a full roster of participating employees and the recurring FICA reduction becomes meaningful. For a fuller walkthrough of the employer mechanics, see [how FICA savings work for employers](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

The key point for Missouri employers is that none of this changes gross compensation or take-home structure beyond the elected benefit. It simply moves qualified dollars out of the taxable wage base, which is exactly what the Internal Revenue Code permits a cafeteria plan to do.

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A Section 125 plan can shelter a defined set of qualified benefits. These include health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts and dependent care flexible spending accounts; health savings account contributions; and dependent care assistance. Long-term care insurance generally does not qualify to run through a cafeteria plan. Each category has its own rules on limits and eligibility, so review the specifics before setting up elections. For the full breakdown of qualifying and non-qualifying benefits, see [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).
