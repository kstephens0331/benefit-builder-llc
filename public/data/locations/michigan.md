## How Section 125 lowers payroll tax in Michigan

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, which shrinks the wages that payroll taxes are calculated on. For Michigan employers, this matters in a specific way: Michigan levies a flat 4.05% state income tax with no standard deduction, so the tax reaches the very first dollar of wages an employee earns. There is no zero-rate floor to absorb a pre-tax election. Every dollar moved into a qualified benefit comes straight off the base for that 4.05% tax.

The same election also reduces the federal income tax base. Federal income tax is graduated, with 2026 single brackets of 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%, applied after a standard deduction of $16,100. On top of that sits FICA, which totals 7.65%: Social Security at 6.2% up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus Medicare at 1.45% on all wages. Both the employer and the employee pay 7.65% each. A qualified pre-tax election under a cafeteria plan leaves the wage base for FICA, federal income tax, and the 4.05% Michigan tax. The employee keeps more take-home pay, and the employer saves its matching 7.65% FICA on every dollar that runs through the plan.

That employer FICA savings is the part many Michigan employers overlook. Because the employer share of FICA is a true cost that disappears when wages are reduced pre-tax, a cafeteria plan turns benefit spending into a payroll tax reduction rather than an added expense. Suppose an employee elects $300 a month, or $3,600 a year, into qualified pre-tax benefits. The employer saves 7.65% of that $3,600, which is $275.40 per year in reduced FICA for that one employee. The same $3,600 also leaves the base for the 4.05% Michigan tax and for the employee's federal income tax and FICA, so the worker's take-home pay rises as well. Multiply the employer side across a workforce and the savings compound quickly. ## What benefits can run pre-tax

A Section 125 plan can carry several categories of pre-tax benefits for Michigan employers. The common ones are health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts (FSAs) and dependent care FSAs; contributions to a health savings account (HSA) made through the plan; and dependent care assistance. Not everything qualifies. Long-term care insurance generally cannot be offered on a pre-tax basis through a cafeteria plan, and certain other benefits are excluded by rule. Getting the menu right is part of keeping the plan compliant and keeping each election eligible for the FICA and income tax treatment described above. For the detailed list of what does and does not qualify, see [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).
