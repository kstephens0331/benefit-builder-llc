## Why Centennial employers use Section 125

Centennial sits in Arapahoe County in the south Denver metro, and its payrolls lean heavily toward technology, professional services, and healthcare employers. These are office and professional workforces: salaried staff, predictable pay cycles, and benefits-conscious employees who compare offers across the Denver metro market. A Section 125 cafeteria plan fits that profile cleanly. It lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, which lowers their taxable wages and, in turn, lowers the employer payroll tax owed on those wages.

The tax math is what makes a cafeteria plan worth running. Every pre-tax dollar an employee routes through the plan escapes federal income tax, Colorado's flat 4.4% state income tax, and the 7.65% FICA tax (Social Security and Medicare). The employee keeps more of each paycheck, and the employer stops paying its matching 7.65% FICA share on the dollars that move into the plan. For a Centennial employer running steady professional payrolls, those FICA savings recur with every pay period and add up across the year. Colorado layers its state income tax on top of the federal burden, so the combined relief for employees is meaningful, and the employer's side of the savings is real money that goes straight back to the bottom line.

A Section 125 plan does not change what an employer already offers. It changes how those benefits get funded, moving qualified premiums and contributions to a pre-tax basis so both sides of the payroll keep more.

## Section 125 for Centennial's technology and professional employers

Technology firms and professional services companies in Centennial run exactly the kind of payroll that a cafeteria plan handles well. Salaried employees on regular pay schedules make pre-tax deductions simple to administer, and there are no irregular wage swings to complicate the withholding. When the plan is set up correctly, the deductions flow through payroll automatically and the tax treatment takes care of itself.

The key variable is participation. The employer's FICA savings scale directly with how many employees enroll and how much they elect on a pre-tax basis. A plan that sits on the shelf saves nothing. A plan that employees understand and use saves the employer its 7.65% FICA share on every pre-tax dollar across the whole participating workforce. That is why education matters as much as the paperwork. When employees see how a pre-tax election affects their own take-home pay, enrollment climbs, and the employer's savings climb with it.
