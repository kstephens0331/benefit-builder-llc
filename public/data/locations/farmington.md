## Why Farmington employers use Section 125

Farmington is the commercial center of San Juan County, anchoring the Four Corners region of northwest New Mexico where New Mexico, Arizona, Colorado, and Utah meet. The local economy spans energy, healthcare, education, and tribal and community employers, including the nearby Navajo Nation communities. These payrolls mix hourly field crews, clinical staff, school employees, and administrative teams, and that variety is exactly where a Section 125 cafeteria plan earns its keep.

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars. When a worker elects coverage, that election comes out of gross pay before federal income tax and before FICA. The employee keeps more take-home pay on the same salary, and the employer cuts its own payroll cost because the 7.65% FICA match is calculated on the reduced wage base. For Farmington employers running tight margins in energy and community services, that FICA reduction recurs on every payroll, every period.

The savings stack because the tax burden does. Wages here carry the 7.65% FICA load and federal income tax, and New Mexico's graduated state income tax adds another layer on top. Every dollar an employee shifts into pre-tax benefits is a dollar removed from all three. Employers in Aztec, Bloomfield, Kirtland, and Shiprock face the same math, which is why pre-tax benefits travel well across San Juan County and the wider Four Corners.
