## Why Cape Girardeau employers use Section 125

Cape Girardeau is the seat of Cape Girardeau County and sits on the Mississippi River in southeast Missouri, serving as a regional hub for the surrounding Bootheel and Ozark-foothills area. Because it draws workers and trade from a wide swath of the region, the local economy runs on schools, healthcare and senior care providers, manufacturers, and a deep base of small businesses. These are steady-payroll employers, and that is exactly where a Section 125 cafeteria plan pays off.

A Section 125 plan lets employees pay for eligible benefits with pre-tax dollars. When an employee makes a pre-tax election, the amount comes out before federal income tax, before Missouri state income tax, and before the 7.65% FICA payroll tax is calculated. That raises the employee's take-home pay on the same gross wages. At the same time, the employer's share of FICA drops, because the employer pays FICA on a smaller taxable wage base. For Cape Girardeau employers running regular weekly or biweekly payrolls, those savings repeat every single pay period, and once the plan is in place there is no added office work to capture them.

The result is a benefit that costs the employer nothing extra to offer. Cape Girardeau employers are not raising wages or adding a line item to the budget. They are restructuring dollars already flowing through payroll so that less is lost to payroll tax. For a manufacturer with a large hourly crew, a school district with classified and certified staff, or a senior care provider staffing multiple shifts, the cumulative FICA savings across a full year can be meaningful.

The same math holds for employers in neighboring Jackson, Scott City, and Sikeston. Any business with W-2 employees and a consistent payroll can put a cafeteria plan to work and keep more of every dollar it already spends on wages.
