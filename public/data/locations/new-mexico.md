## How Section 125 lowers payroll tax in New Mexico

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars. The money an employee elects comes out of gross wages before income and payroll taxes are calculated, so both the worker and the employer keep more of every dollar. For New Mexico employers, the savings stack across three layers of tax.

New Mexico levies a graduated state income tax that ranges from 1.7% on the lowest bracket up to 5.9% on higher earnings, paired with a standard deduction of $16,100. Because most working households fall into the middle of that schedule, a typical worker's pre-tax election avoids state income tax in roughly the 4.7% to 4.9% range. Federal income tax is also graduated, with 2026 single brackets of 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37% and a standard deduction of $16,100. On top of income tax sits FICA, which totals 7.65%: Social Security at 6.2% up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus Medicare at 1.45% with no cap. The employer matches that 7.65%, and the employee pays the same.

A qualified pre-tax election lowers the wage base used for FICA, federal income tax, and New Mexico state income tax all at once. The employee sees a smaller tax bill and a larger net paycheck. The employer sees its matching 7.65% FICA obligation shrink on every dollar that runs through the plan. That employer FICA reduction is the recurring, mechanical benefit of administering a compliant cafeteria plan.

Here is a clearly hypothetical illustration. Suppose an employee elects $300 a month, which is $3,600 a year, into qualified pre-tax benefits. The employer saves its 7.65% FICA on that amount: $3,600 multiplied by 7.65% equals $275.40 in employer payroll tax savings for that single employee in one year. Multiply that across a roster and the employer side savings grow with participation. The employee separately avoids federal income tax, the 6.2% and 1.45% employee share of FICA, and New Mexico state income tax on the same $3,600. These figures are illustrative only and depend on each worker's wages, filing status, and elections. Benefit Builder does not set or guarantee any fixed monthly benefit amount; the right election is whatever the employee chooses for qualified coverage. For a deeper walkthrough of the employer side, see [how FICA savings work for employers](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A cafeteria plan covers a defined set of qualified benefits. Common pre-tax elections include health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts (FSAs) and dependent care FSAs; health savings account (HSA) contributions; and dependent care assistance. Most group benefits that protect health or care obligations qualify. Long-term care insurance generally does not qualify for pre-tax treatment under Section 125, so it is handled outside the plan. For a fuller list of what is and is not eligible, see [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).
