## Why Houston employers use Section 125

Houston is the seat of Harris County and the largest city in Texas, anchoring one of the most diverse regional economies in the country. Energy, healthcare, manufacturing, and logistics all run deep here, and beneath those headline industries sits a broad base of small and mid-size businesses that handle everything from professional services to skilled trades. That mix is exactly why a Section 125 cafeteria plan fits Houston employers of nearly every shape and size: the plan works the same whether a company runs a single office or staffs hundreds of workers across multiple sites.

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, which lowers the wages subject to payroll tax for both sides of the paycheck. Because Texas has no state income tax, Houston employers do not lose savings to a state withholding line the way employers in many other states do. The savings instead come from two clean sources: federal income tax on the employee side, and the 7.65 percent FICA that the employer and the employee both stop paying on every dollar routed through the plan. For an employer, that 7.65 percent reduction in matching FICA is real money returned to the business on each pay period, and it scales directly with how many employees participate.

Pre-tax benefits also make a compensation package more competitive in a tight Houston labor market without raising gross payroll. Employees keep more of what they earn, and the employer trims its payroll tax burden at the same time. That alignment is what makes a cafeteria plan one of the few benefit moves that helps the employer and the workforce in the same motion.

## Section 125 for large and small Houston workforces

The economics of Section 125 scale with participation. The more employees who route eligible dollars through the plan, the larger the total pool of wages that drops out of the FICA base, and the larger the employer's matching FICA savings becomes. For a large Houston workforce, that compounding effect can add up to a substantial annual figure, because the 7.65 percent the employer avoids applies across every enrolled paycheck. A hospital system, a logistics operation, or a manufacturing plant with a deep roster often sees the biggest total employer FICA savings simply because the headcount is there.

Small Houston offices benefit just as cleanly. The math is identical per employee, so a ten-person professional services firm captures the same per-paycheck FICA relief that a large employer does, just over a smaller base. There is no minimum size at which a cafeteria plan suddenly starts working; it works from the first enrolled employee forward.

What separates a plan that delivers from one that underperforms is participation, and participation depends on two things being handled well: payroll coordination and employee education. Deductions have to be set up correctly and synced with each pay run, and employees have to understand what the plan does for their take-home pay before they will elect into it.
