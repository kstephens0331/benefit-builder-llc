## How Section 125 lowers payroll tax in Oklahoma

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars. When an employee makes a qualified election, that amount is removed from gross wages before federal income tax, Oklahoma state income tax, and FICA are calculated. Lower taxable wages mean lower tax for the worker and lower payroll tax for Oklahoma employers.

Oklahoma uses a graduated state income tax with brackets that top out at 4.75 percent, paired with a standard deduction of $6,520. For most workers, a pre-tax election lands in roughly the 3.75 percent to 4.75 percent marginal range, so each dollar moved into the cafeteria plan avoids state tax at that rate. Federal income tax is also graduated. The 2026 single brackets are 10 percent, 12 percent, 22 percent, 24 percent, 32 percent, 35 percent, and 37 percent, with a standard deduction of $16,100. Where a given dollar of pay falls determines the federal rate it would otherwise carry.

FICA is the piece that makes Section 125 valuable for the employer. FICA totals 7.65 percent, made up of Social Security at 6.2 percent up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500 plus Medicare at 1.45 percent. Both the employee and the employer each pay 7.65 percent. Because a qualified pre-tax election is removed from wages for FICA, the employer no longer owes its 7.65 percent share on that amount. That saving is automatic, recurs every pay period, and grows with each enrolled employee.

Here is a clearly hypothetical example to show the mechanics. Suppose an employee elects $300 a month, which is $3,600 a year, into qualified pre-tax benefits. The employer FICA saving is the election times 7.65 percent, or $3,600 times 0.0765, which equals $275.40 per year for that one employee. The employee also avoids federal income tax at their marginal federal rate and Oklahoma state income tax in roughly the 3.75 percent to 4.75 percent range on the same $3,600, so take-home pay rises while the cost of the benefit falls. Multiply the employer side across a full roster and the payroll tax reduction becomes a meaningful line item. This is illustrative only and not a fixed Benefit Builder benefit amount. For a deeper walkthrough of the employer side, see [how FICA savings work for employers](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A cafeteria plan can run a range of common benefits on a pre-tax basis. These include health, dental, and vision insurance premiums, health flexible spending accounts and dependent care flexible spending accounts, contributions to a health savings account, and dependent care assistance. Each of these reduces taxable wages for the employee and reduces FICA wages for the employer. Long-term care insurance generally does not qualify to run through a Section 125 plan, so it is handled separately. For a fuller list of what does and does not qualify, see [which benefits qualify for Section 125](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).
