For brokers and agents, a Section 125 plan is an easy value-add to bring to employer clients, but only if the compliance side is handled correctly. The savings are straightforward. The paperwork is where plans get into trouble. This checklist covers what a compliant Section 125 plan needs, so you can speak to it with confidence and spot a plan that is missing pieces.

## 1. A written plan document

Section 125 requires a written plan document. It defines the benefits offered, who is eligible, the plan year, and the election rules. Pre-tax treatment without a written plan document is not valid, even if the payroll deductions are running correctly. This is the single most common gap in plans that were set up informally.

## 2. An adoption agreement

The adoption agreement is the formal action by which the employer establishes the plan and names the plan administrator. It is the board-ready document that ties the plan to the company. A broker reviewing an existing plan should confirm it exists and is signed.

## 3. Salary reduction agreements

Each participating employee must have a salary reduction agreement on file. This is the employee's election to redirect part of their pay to a qualified benefit on a pre-tax basis. These are collected at enrollment and at each qualifying life event or open enrollment change. They are the evidence that the pre-tax election was authorized.

## 4. A summary plan description

Employees need a summary plan description that explains the plan in plain language: what is offered, how to elect, and how the rules work. Good employee communication also drives participation, which is what makes the plan valuable to the employer. A plan your client cannot explain to their staff will underperform.

## 5. Annual nondiscrimination testing

Section 125 plans must pass annual nondiscrimination testing to confirm the plan does not disproportionately favor highly compensated or key employees. There are three core test families: an eligibility test, a contributions and benefits test, and a key employee concentration test. Specific components like a Dependent Care FSA carry additional tests. We explain the tests in [our NDT guide for agents](/blog/nondiscrimination-testing-explained-for-agents).

Testing is not a one-time setup item. It runs every plan year, and a plan that trends toward failure should be flagged during the year, not at the audit.

## 6. An audit trail

The documents above only protect the plan if they can be produced on request. A clean plan keeps signed agreements, testing results, and plan documents organized and retrievable for an IRS or Department of Labor inquiry.
