A Section 125 plan, also called a cafeteria plan, is a written employee benefit plan authorized by Section 125 of the Internal Revenue Code. It lets employees pay for certain qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars through payroll. Because those dollars come out before income and payroll taxes are calculated, the employee keeps more of each paycheck and the employer lowers its payroll tax bill. This guide explains how that works, in plain language, for employers who are evaluating a plan.

## What "pre-tax" actually means

When an employee elects a benefit under a Section 125 plan, they agree to a salary reduction. Their gross pay on paper stays the same, but the elected amount is redirected to the benefit before taxes are figured. That reduces the wages subject to federal income tax, Social Security tax, and Medicare tax, and in most states it reduces state income tax too.

The result is simple. The employee pays for the same benefit they would have bought anyway, but with untaxed dollars, so their take-home pay goes up. You can see the effect on your own roster with our [savings calculator](/savings-calculator).

## Which benefits qualify

Section 125 is a delivery mechanism, not a benefit by itself. The plan is the pre-tax wrapper around qualified benefits, which commonly include:

- Group health, dental, and vision premiums
- Health Savings Account (HSA) contributions
- Health Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA)
- Dependent Care Assistance (Dependent Care FSA)
- Group term life insurance, within IRS limits

A Premium Only Plan, or POP, is the simplest version. It runs only insurance premiums through the pre-tax wrapper and is a common starting point for small and midsize employers.

## Why employers offer it

The employer benefit is direct. Every dollar an employee runs pre-tax is a dollar that is no longer part of the wage base for the employer share of Social Security and Medicare tax. That employer share is 7.65% of wages, so the plan lowers payroll tax on every pre-tax dollar across the whole participating group. For many employers the payroll tax savings more than cover the cost of administering the plan.

Beyond the math, a Section 125 plan strengthens the benefits package. Employees notice higher take-home pay, and a clean pre-tax structure makes voluntary and supplemental coverage easier to offer.

## What a compliant plan requires

A Section 125 plan is a formal IRS plan, not a payroll setting. To be valid it needs a written plan document, an adoption agreement, salary reduction agreements signed by participating employees, a summary plan description for employees, and annual nondiscrimination testing to confirm the plan does not favor highly compensated or key employees. We cover the testing in detail in our [compliance overview](/compliance).

Skipping the paperwork is the most common mistake. Without the documents and testing, the pre-tax treatment is not defensible in an audit. A good administrator handles all of it so the employer does not have to.
