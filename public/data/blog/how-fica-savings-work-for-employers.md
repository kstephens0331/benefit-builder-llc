Most employers know a Section 125 plan saves money, but the mechanism is worth understanding precisely, because it is what makes the plan pay for itself. The savings come from FICA, the federal payroll tax that funds Social Security and Medicare. This article walks through exactly how a pre-tax benefit shrinks your FICA bill.

## The 7.65% employer share

FICA is split into two taxes. Social Security is 6.2% of wages up to an annual wage base, and Medicare is 1.45% of all wages. Added together that is 7.65%. Both the employee and the employer pay that 7.65%, so on a normal payroll the government collects 15.3% of wages in FICA before income tax is even considered.

The employer share is the part you control with a Section 125 plan. Every dollar of wages you remove from the FICA wage base removes 7.65 cents of employer payroll tax.

## How a pre-tax election removes wages from the base

When an employee makes a pre-tax election under a Section 125 plan, that amount is a salary reduction. It is excluded from the wages used to calculate FICA. The employee no longer pays their 7.65% on it, and you no longer pay your 7.65% on it.

Consider a simple example. An employee elects 200 dollars a month in pre-tax benefits, which is 2,400 dollars a year. At the 7.65% employer rate, that single employee lowers your payroll tax by about 184 dollars a year. Multiply that across a participating group and the numbers add up quickly. ## The savings scale with participation

Because the savings come from each participating employee, the total grows with enrollment. This is why clear employee communication matters. A plan that is well explained gets higher participation, and higher participation means more payroll tax savings for the employer. A plan that is poorly communicated leaves savings on the table.

It is also why the employer and employee interests line up. The same pre-tax election that raises an employee's take-home pay lowers the employer's payroll tax. Both sides win on the same dollar.

## What the savings do and do not cover

The employer FICA savings are real, but they are not unlimited. Social Security tax only applies up to the annual wage base, so for very high earners the Social Security portion may already be maxed out and only the 1.45% Medicare portion is saved on additional pre-tax dollars. For the large majority of employees, the full 7.65% applies.

The savings also depend on the benefit being a qualified pre-tax benefit under Section 125 and on the plan being properly documented and tested. Pre-tax treatment that is not backed by a compliant plan is not defensible. Our [compliance overview](/compliance) covers what a valid plan requires.

## Turning the savings into a decision

For most employers the practical question is whether the payroll tax savings exceed the cost of running the plan. For a typical group they do, often by a wide margin, because the administration fee is a small percentage while the FICA savings apply to every pre-tax dollar across the whole group.
