If you have looked into Section 125 at all, you have probably seen the term Premium Only Plan, often shortened to POP. It is the simplest form of cafeteria plan, and for many small and mid-size employers it is the right place to start. This article explains what a POP does, who it fits, and what it takes to run one.

## What a Premium Only Plan does

A Premium Only Plan does exactly one thing, and it does it well. It lets employees pay their share of insurance premiums before tax. That is the entire scope. There is no flexible spending account to administer, no dependent care tracking, just the premium dollars employees are already paying, now treated pre-tax.

Because the premium is taken out before income tax and FICA are calculated, the employee takes home more pay for the same coverage, and the employer lowers its payroll tax on those dollars. We walk through that mechanism in [how FICA savings work](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

## Who a POP fits

A Premium Only Plan is a strong fit for an employer who offers insurance and wants the tax advantage without the complexity of a full cafeteria plan. It is common for a smaller business, a first time plan sponsor, or any employer who wants a clean, low maintenance way to capture the premium savings.

It is also a natural starting point. Many employers begin with a POP and add flexible spending accounts or other qualifying benefits later, once the plan is established and the team is comfortable with it. If you want to see the full menu of what can be added, see [which benefits qualify](/blog/which-benefits-qualify-for-section-125).

## What a POP requires

A Premium Only Plan is simple, but it is still a formal benefit plan, which means it needs real documentation. That includes a written plan document and adoption agreement that establish the plan, salary reduction agreements that record each employee's election, and the nondiscrimination testing the code requires to confirm the plan does not unfairly favor higher paid employees.

None of that is optional. A POP that exists only as an informal payroll practice, with no plan document behind it, does not actually provide defensible pre-tax treatment. The simplicity of a POP is in its scope, not in skipping the paperwork. Our [compliance overview](/compliance) covers exactly what a valid plan requires.

## The savings

The savings work the same way they do in any Section 125 plan. Every premium dollar moved pre-tax is removed from the wage base used to calculate FICA, so the employer saves its 7.65% share and the employee saves their share plus income tax.

Consider a simple example. An employee paying 150 dollars a month in premiums, or 1,800 dollars a year, moves that amount pre-tax. At the 7.65% employer rate that lowers the employer's payroll tax by about 138 dollars a year for that one employee. Across a participating group the savings add up. You can model your own roster with the [savings calculator](/savings-calculator).

## POP versus a full cafeteria plan

The difference is scope. A Premium Only Plan covers premiums and nothing else. A full cafeteria plan can also include health and dependent care flexible spending accounts, health savings account contributions, and other qualifying benefits. The full plan captures more savings because it covers more of an employee's real spending, but it also has more moving parts to administer.

The right choice depends on what you offer and how much you want to manage. Many employers find that starting with a POP and expanding later is the lowest friction path.
