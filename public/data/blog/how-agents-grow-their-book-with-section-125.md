Most insurance agents already sell the products that pair naturally with a Section 125 plan, accident, cancer, hospital indemnity, critical illness, and supplemental life at the worksite. What many agents do not do is bring the pre-tax plan that makes those products easier to place. This article explains how adding Section 125 helps an agent win and keep more cases, without taking on the administration.

## Pre-tax treatment makes every product an easier yes

When the benefits you present are paid pre-tax, the math changes in your favor. The employee takes home more pay for the same coverage, and the employer lowers its payroll tax on those dollars. A product that looked like a cost a moment ago now comes with a built in tax advantage for both sides of the table.

That is a different conversation. Instead of asking an employer to absorb a new expense, you are showing them a way to give employees more value while the business saves on payroll tax. The same election that raises an employee's take-home pay lowers the employer's FICA, which we explain in [how FICA savings work](/blog/how-fica-savings-work-for-employers).

## You keep your commissions

This is the part agents most want to hear. Partnering on the Section 125 side does not change your relationship with the carrier or your compensation. You stay the agent of record and you keep 100 percent of your commissions. The pre-tax plan is an administration service the employer pays for, not a competitor to your products.

## The admin is handled

The reason most agents skip pre-tax benefits is the administration. Plan documents, salary reduction agreements, annual nondiscrimination testing, payroll coordination, recordkeeping, it looks like a burden, and it is not what an agent is in business to do.

That is exactly the part a Section 125 administrator takes off your plate. The compliance checklist that would otherwise be your problem becomes someone else's job. If you want to see the full scope of what that involves, it is laid out in the [Section 125 compliance checklist](/blog/section-125-compliance-checklist-for-brokers), and the testing piece specifically in [nondiscrimination testing explained](/blog/nondiscrimination-testing-explained-for-agents).

## Participation and persistency

There is a second benefit that shows up after the sale. A pre-tax plan that is well explained gets higher participation, because employees understand they are getting more take-home pay. Higher participation means more of your placed products are actually elected and kept, which improves persistency. A case that enrolls well and stays enrolled is worth more to you than one that lapses.

Good employee education is part of running the plan, and it works in your favor by lifting the very enrollment your compensation depends on.

## Where it fits in your sales process

The cleanest place to introduce Section 125 is early, when you are first scoping an employer's benefits. Position it as the structure that makes everything else more attractive, then bring in the administrator to handle the plan while you focus on the products and the relationship. You do not need to explain the full compliance picture yourself, that is what the administrator is there for.
